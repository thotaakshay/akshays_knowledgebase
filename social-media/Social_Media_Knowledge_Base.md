# 0. OPERATING CONTRACT FOR THIS FILE

This section controls how the knowledge base must be used and updated.

## 0.1 Canonical-source rule
Before giving advice on any Reel, Instagram post, caption, cover, upload decision, profile change, content strategy, audience-growth question, social-media monetization question, or performance review, read this entire file first.

Do not rely only on memory of earlier chats. Do not jump directly to generic advice such as “use trending audio,” “post consistently,” or “add hashtags.” The recommendation must be based on Akshay's actual creator identity, the specific piece of content, the intended audience feeling, the platform, and the current stage of the account.

## 0.2 Relationship with the Solo Filmmaker Knowledge Base
These two knowledge bases have different jobs.

**Solo Filmmaker KB:** How should the film be made?

**Social Media KB:** How should the film be introduced to the world, who should receive it, why should they care, what should be changed before upload, how should it be packaged, what should be learned from performance, and how can the audience eventually support a business?

When Akshay says he is going to upload a Reel, both systems may be relevant.

Use the Solo Filmmaker KB when a visual, pacing, performance, sound, framing, continuity, or editing problem still needs to be corrected.

Use this Social Media KB to judge audience connection, positioning, packaging, distribution, profile fit, engagement, growth, analytics, and monetization.

Never allow social-media optimization to destroy a film's intention merely to chase a generic trend. If a proposed change improves reach but weakens Akshay's identity or the emotional experience, state the tradeoff explicitly.

## 0.3 Mutable-truth rule
This is a living knowledge base, not an append-only diary.

If a rule is later proven wrong, incomplete, outdated, or harmful:

1. Find the old rule in its correct section.
2. Replace or remove the wrong wording.
3. Add the corrected rule in the same logical location.
4. Add a short Change Log entry explaining what changed and why.
5. Do not leave contradictory current rules active at the same time.

Historical discoveries can remain inside the Evidence / Experiment Log if they are useful, but they must not be mistaken for current rules.

## 0.4 Stable principle vs changing platform fact
This file contains two different kinds of knowledge.

### Stable strategic knowledge
Examples:
- Akshay wants recognition for imagination and filmmaking.
- The audience should feel something, not merely receive information.
- His page is not primarily a filmmaking-tutorial page.
- His page is not primarily a motivational-quote page.
- Travel, work, fitness, relationships, failures, beauty, freedom, and growth can all exist inside the same creator identity.

These do not need to be re-researched every time.

### Changing platform knowledge
Examples:
- current recommendation signals;
- current Instagram feature availability;
- Trial Reel behavior;
- maximum Reel length;
- current monetization programs;
- Creator Marketplace availability;
- music licensing or branded-content rules;
- current best practices published by Instagram/Meta;
- current distribution restrictions;
- current analytics surfaces.

Before making a factual recommendation that depends on one of these changing items, verify current reliable sources. Prefer Instagram/Meta's current official guidance. Do not freeze a 2026 platform tactic into a permanent truth.

## 0.5 Evidence-before-rule rule
Every new social-media discovery should be classified as one of:

- **Observed evidence:** what actually happened on Akshay's account/content.
- **Working hypothesis:** what may explain the result.
- **Verified personal rule:** repeated evidence strongly suggests this works for Akshay's audience/content.
- **Platform rule:** current documented behavior from a reliable platform source.
- **Creative identity rule:** a deliberate decision about what Akshay wants his work to mean.
- **Open question:** unresolved and needing a test.

A single Reel performing well or badly is not enough to create a universal rule.

## 0.6 No vanity-metric rule
Do not judge a Reel only by raw views.

A Reel with many views but weak connection, no profile interest, no follows, no shares, no saves, and no identity recognition may be less useful than a smaller Reel that attracts the exact audience Akshay wants.

Always ask what job the upload was supposed to perform.

## 0.7 No-perfection-before-publishing rule
The purpose of this system is to improve publishing decisions, not create another reason to endlessly delay publishing.

Before upload, separate issues into:

- **Must fix:** likely harms understanding, quality, trust, platform suitability, or creator identity.
- **Worth fixing:** meaningful improvement if the cost is reasonable.
- **Optional polish:** small improvement that should not delay release.
- **Future learning:** useful for the next Reel, not a reason to rebuild the current one.

This distinction is mandatory whenever reviewing a Reel.

# 2. TARGET AUDIENCE

## 2.1 Primary audience concept — Cinematic Dreamers
The primary audience is not defined only by age, city, gender, or profession. It is defined first by psychology.

These are people who may:

- love movies, series, beautiful cinematography, music, edits, and emotionally strong scenes;
- imagine their own life as if it were a film;
- turn music into imaginary scenes in their head;
- feel bored by repetitive routine but still believe life can become bigger;
- want to improve themselves physically, mentally, professionally, creatively, or emotionally;
- want to travel, meet people, see the world, and collect experiences;
- enjoy fitness, lifestyle, fashion, travel, technology, culture, or personal-growth content;
- save or share Reels that express something they feel but cannot easily say;
- follow creators whose life feels aspirational yet human;
- want a short emotional escape while scrolling;
- appreciate beauty in ordinary life;
- feel motivated more by seeing someone live than by being lectured;
- want to watch someone's journey unfold over time.

## 2.2 Audience emotional needs
Akshay's content can serve several emotional needs.

### Escape
“Give me 20–40 seconds away from my boring scroll.”

### Recognition
“Someone else feels what I feel.”

### Aspiration
“I want my own life to feel like this.”

### Beauty
“I never noticed ordinary life could look this meaningful.”

### Momentum
“This makes me want to get up and do something.”

### Curiosity
“What will happen in his life next?”

### Connection
“I understand this person even though I have never met him.”

## 2.3 Secondary audiences
Secondary audiences can include:

- filmmakers and creators who admire the craft;
- people interested in solo iPhone filmmaking;
- travelers;
- fitness/self-development audiences;
- young professionals who feel routine is swallowing their ambition;
- brands looking for cinematic creator storytelling.

These groups are valuable, but the work should not become so technical that the primary emotional audience feels excluded.

## 2.4 Audience recognition test
Before upload ask:

> **Can a stranger who does not know Akshay see some part of themselves, their dream, their feeling, their fear, or their desired life inside this Reel?**

If yes, connection potential is high.

If the Reel only makes sense because Akshay personally remembers what happened, the story may need additional context.

# 8. REEL UPLOAD REVIEW PROTOCOL — MANDATORY

Whenever Akshay says anything equivalent to:

- “I am going to upload this Reel.”
- “Should I post this?”
- “Give caption/hashtags for this.”
- “How can I get more views?”
- “Check this before I upload.”

follow this protocol.

## 8.1 Step 1 — Actually inspect the content
If the Reel/video is available, inspect the real export or footage before giving packaging advice.

Do not generate a caption from the title alone when the video itself is available.

Check:

- total duration;
- first visible frame;
- first 1–3 seconds;
- story/emotional progression;
- visual quality;
- pacing;
- text overlays;
- audio/music relationship;
- final frame;
- accidental export screens/watermarks;
- orientation/aspect ratio;
- whether any important information is unclear;
- whether the ending earns its duration;
- whether the Reel represents Akshay's intended identity.

If a filmmaking defect materially affects the upload, use the Solo Filmmaker KB to diagnose it.

## 8.2 Step 2 — State the Reel's actual job
Classify the Reel primarily as:

- discovery;
- connection;
- event;
- portfolio/skill demonstration;
- experiment;
- brand/commercial;
- mixed purpose.

Do not optimize every Reel for exactly the same behavior.

## 8.3 Step 3 — Identify the audience entry point
Answer:

- Why would a stranger care?
- What universal feeling, desire, question, tension, beauty, or curiosity gives them entry?
- Is that entry visible inside the Reel, or does packaging need to provide it?

## 8.4 Step 4 — First-seconds test
Ask:

- Is the opening visually strong enough to stop attention?
- Is the situation understandable?
- Does mystery create curiosity, or merely confusion?
- Does text help, or does it explain too much?
- Would a stronger first frame exist later in the Reel?
- Can we improve the opening without betraying the film's intention?

A hook is not automatically loud text, a shock, or clickbait. A hook can be beauty, action, tension, a face, an unusual composition, sound, a question, a recognizable situation, or a line of text that gives emotional meaning.

## 8.5 Step 5 — Audience-feeling map
Describe what the intended viewer is likely to feel at key moments.

Example structure:

**Opening:** curiosity / recognition  
**Middle:** immersion / identification  
**Shift:** aspiration / emotion  
**Ending:** reflection / desire to rewatch / desire to see next chapter

If the Reel has no emotional movement, say so.

## 8.6 Step 6 — Identity fit
Ask:

- Does this show Akshay's imagination?
- Does this strengthen “cinematic life storytelling”?
- Would a new visitor understand why this creator is different?
- Is it merely aesthetically pleasing, or does it carry a point of view?
- Does it belong beside his previous/future work?

Not every Reel must be profound. But repeated uploads should accumulate identity.

## 8.7 Step 7 — Pre-upload mistakes
Classify every issue:

### MUST FIX
Examples:
- accidental editor outro;
- distracting watermark;
- unreadable/cropped text;
- wrong export orientation that seriously damages mobile viewing;
- broken audio;
- severe pacing dead zone;
- misleading caption;
- visible continuity/edit error that ruins understanding;
- copyrighted/brand/legal issue needing correction;
- an opening that makes the story unintelligible when a simple correction exists.

### WORTH FIXING
Examples:
- stronger cover available;
- a slightly better opening trim;
- unnecessary second of dead time;
- text can be clearer;
- caption can create a much stronger emotional entry;
- vertical reframe could materially improve experience.

### OPTIONAL POLISH
Examples:
- tiny grade adjustment;
- minor text-position refinement;
- alternate hashtag choice;
- tiny cover typography change.

### FUTURE LEARNING
Examples:
- next time capture a stronger ending;
- next time design more separation;
- next time capture a reaction insert;
- next time shoot a vertical-safe alternate composition.

Never turn Future Learning into a reason not to publish the current Reel.

## 8.8 Step 8 — Packaging
After the Reel itself passes review, provide:

- opening text only if useful;
- caption;
- cover recommendation;
- Reel title/working title if useful;
- hashtags/keywords where useful;
- location/tag suggestions if genuinely relevant;
- collaborator/tag suggestions only when authentic;
- Story support plan;
- pinned-comment idea only if it adds value;
- upload-format recommendation;
- Trial Reel recommendation when currently available and strategically appropriate.

Do not mechanically provide every element if the Reel does not need it.

## 8.9 Step 9 — Expected connection
Explain in plain English:

- which audience segment may connect;
- why they may connect;
- what part may be shareable;
- what may make someone follow;
- what may limit reach or connection.

Do not promise views or virality.

## 8.10 Step 10 — Post-upload measurement plan
Before publishing, decide what success means for this specific Reel.

Examples:

- Discovery Reel → non-follower reach, shares, profile visits, follows, watch behavior.
- Connection Reel → comments, replies, saves, repeat viewers, Story responses.
- Portfolio Reel → profile visits, creator/brand interest, quality of comments, shares by creative people.
- Experiment → controlled comparison against another version.

# 9. CAPTION SYSTEM

## 9.1 Caption's job
The caption should not merely describe what viewers can already see.

A caption can:

- reveal the thought behind the film;
- give strangers an emotional entry point;
- add context the visual deliberately leaves unstated;
- create identification;
- deepen the ending;
- invite a real conversation;
- help discovery through natural relevant language.

## 9.2 Caption tone
Default tone:

- simple English;
- human;
- reflective when appropriate;
- not fake-deep;
- not corporate;
- not motivational-speaker language;
- not overloaded with emojis;
- not stuffed with hashtags;
- not trying desperately to go viral.

Akshay's own voice should gradually become recognizable.

## 9.3 Caption levels
### Minimal
One short line when the film already says almost everything.

### Reflective
A few sentences revealing what Akshay felt or noticed.

### Story context
A short paragraph when the audience needs the real-life situation to understand the film more deeply.

### Process/BTS
Useful when the making itself is part of the story, especially after viewers admire the result.

## 9.4 Never force the lesson
If a film makes someone feel inspired, that is enough. It does not need to end with:

“Therefore you should work hard and never give up.”

Show more. Lecture less.

# 10. HASHTAG AND DISCOVERY-METADATA SYSTEM

## 10.1 Hashtags are supporting metadata, not the creative strategy
Never act as though hashtags can rescue weak content.

Use relevant, specific tags only when useful.

Potential categories:

- content form: cinematicreels, shortfilm, visualstorytelling;
- craft: shotoniphone, solofilmmaking;
- subject: travel, gym, worklife, Hyderabad, etc.;
- theme: selfgrowth, lifestyle, etc., only when genuinely represented.

Do not blindly paste the same large hashtag block onto every post.

## 10.2 Natural language matters
Use clear words in captions/on-screen text when they naturally describe the content. This helps humans first and may also help platform understanding/search.
Do not write awkward keyword-stuffed captions.

## 10.3 Location
Use a location when place genuinely contributes to discovery or the story.

Do not attach random popular locations to chase reach.

## 10.4 Tags and collaborators
Tag only:

- actual collaborators;
- relevant locations/brands when naturally involved;
- people who meaningfully belong to the piece.

Do not spam large accounts hoping for attention.

# 11. COVER SYSTEM

A cover has two jobs:

1. help the profile grid look intentional;
2. make a profile visitor understand or want to open the Reel.

Choose a frame with:

- clear subject;
- readable composition at small size;
- strong emotion or visual curiosity;
- room for text if text is needed.

Cover text should be short. It should not turn every Reel into a YouTube-thumbnail aesthetic unless that becomes a deliberate creative choice.

The best frame inside the film is not automatically the best cover because covers are seen small and out of context.

# 19. EXPERIMENT SYSTEM

Do not change five variables and then pretend we learned why a Reel performed differently.

When testing, isolate one meaningful variable when practical.

Examples:

- opening frame A vs B;
- no opening text vs one-line context;
- shorter vs longer opening hold;
- one cover vs another;
- Trial Reel vs normal release when appropriate;
- short reflective caption vs contextual caption.

Record:

- hypothesis;
- what changed;
- what stayed constant;
- outcome;
- possible explanation;

# 23. PRE-PUBLISH GATE — SHORT VERSION

Before saying **POST IT**, answer all of these:

1. **Purpose:** What job does this upload perform?
2. **Audience:** Who can see themselves or their desire in it?
3. **Feeling:** What should they feel?
4. **Opening:** Why would a stranger stop?
5. **Clarity:** Is useful mystery being mistaken for confusion?
6. **Identity:** Does this strengthen Akshay's creator world?
7. **Craft:** Is there any important visual/edit/sound problem still fixable?
8. **Platform:** Is the export appropriate for Instagram consumption?
9. **Ending:** Does the final moment finish the experience cleanly?
10. **Packaging:** Caption, cover, text, tags, location—only what helps.
11. **Connection:** Why might someone send/save/follow?
12. **Commercial fit:** Does this build an audience/portfolio useful for the long-term business?
13. **Perfection check:** Are remaining issues truly important, or are we delaying because it is not perfect?
14. **Measurement:** What will we learn after posting?

If Must Fix issues remain, recommend correcting them first.

If only Optional Polish/Future Learning remains, default toward publishing.
